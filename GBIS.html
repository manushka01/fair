Understanding the Great British Insulation Scheme (GBIS)

The Great British Insulation Scheme (GBIS) is a government initiative designed to help reduce home energy bills by providing support for installing insulation. The scheme aims to enhance energy efficiency, lower carbon emissions, and improve home comfort. Understanding GBIS grant scheme eligibility criteria is crucial for homeowners, landlords, and tenants (including social housing residents) interested in taking advantage of the benefits. and tenants interested in taking advantage of the benefits.

Eligibility Criteria for GBIS

To be eligible for the GBIS, your property must meet specific criteria:

Property Requirements −

Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) Rating

Properties with an EPC rating of D, E, F, or G are eligible. You can check your EPC rating here.

Council Tax Bands

  • In England, properties in Council Tax bands A–D qualify.
  • In Wales, properties in Council Tax bands A–E are eligible.

You can check your Council Tax band here.

Occupant Requirements +

Certain households may also qualify based on benefits received or combined household income, in line with the scheme's flexible eligibility criteria set by the government.

Savings On Your Energy Bills

Insulate your home and reduce heat loss through your walls, floor, and roof.

Roof25%
Walls35%
Floor15%

A home will lose heat through the walls 35%, floor 15%, and roof 25%, with the other 25% lost through areas such as windows and doors. When temperatures are lower or the wind is stronger, more heat is lost. This is why it is important to insulate your home to protect you and your family from cold in the UK climate.

Warmer

A well-insulated home retains more heat. This means that your home stays warmer for longer, reducing the need for constant heating.

Cleaner

A well-insulated home with proper ventilation creates cleaner air quality, reducing damp and mould in your living spaces.

Quieter

A well-insulated home helps to soundproof your home, creating a quieter, more peaceful environment inside.

Types of Insulation Covered

You can receive several measures to help reduce heat loss in your home. This results in a more comfortable environment and lower energy bills.

  • Cavity Wall Insulation: Filling the gaps between walls to reduce heat loss.
  • Internal Wall Insulation: Insulating the internal walls to reduce heat loss.
  • Loft Insulation: Insulating loft spaces to prevent heat from escaping through the roof.
  • Floor Insulation: Insulating floors to reduce heat loss through the ground.
  • Room-in-Roof Insulation: Insulating rooms that are directly under the roof.
